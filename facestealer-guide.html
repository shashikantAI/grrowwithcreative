Fake Zoom / Teams meeting: the FBI's checklist (keyword FACE)

Source: FBI FLASH AC-111226-MS "FaceStealer", 12 Nov 2026 (TLP:CLEAR) — ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261112.pdf

How the scam works

  1. A friend's Telegram / WhatsApp / LinkedIn / Slack account is hacked. You get a meeting invite from it.
  2. The link looks like Zoom or Teams, but the address is different. FBI examples: uu01webzoom.us, teams.livemeet.us
  3. Inside, your "friend" is an old recording. Other "people" are waiting.
  4. "We can't hear you, run this update" → an update, a script, or a command to paste. That is the malware.
  5. Your webcam was recorded from the moment you clicked. Your video is played to YOUR friends in the next fake call.

The FBI's 7 habits

  1. Join with the official app and the meeting code, not by clicking a link.
  2. Keep your camera off until you can see, hear and talk to the person who invited you.
  3. Never run a script or paste code into a terminal to "fix" or "update" a call. Update the app separately.
  4. Check calendar invites: a meeting link that arrives only minutes before the call is a warning sign.
  5. Confirm the meeting with the person on a different channel (phone call or SMS if they messaged on Telegram).
  6. Turn on two-factor login, login alerts and disappearing messages on WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack.
  7. Keep only small amounts in browser wallets; never store seed phrases or private keys on your devices.

Clicked already? The FBI's 6 steps

  1. Move crypto to a NEW wallet with new seed phrases, made on a different, clean device.
  2. Change passwords, log out of all sessions and add two-factor login on WhatsApp, Telegram and work / developer accounts.
  3. Tell your friends and your employer that your account and your webcam video may be used to fool them.
  4. Change all your passwords.
  5. If you lost an account, ask the platform to shut it down or give it back.
  6. Report it. FBI: ic3.gov, keyword "FaceStealer". In India: cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
